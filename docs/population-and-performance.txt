Larger populations and performance
==================================

World supports larger civilian communities through population scaling and
performance improvements. Both features are present; selecting an optional
simulation mode does not itself change the population setting.

Population targets
------------------

Civs + Police Density is the ordinary civilian/street-police budget per 50x50
surface map. The default is 25 and scales with actual district area:

District size    Default target    Target at density 75
30x30            9                 27
50x50            25                75
70x70            49                147
100x100          100               300

These are ordinary surface targets, not a cap on all actors: special residents,
the player, zombies and travel can increase the total. The civilian/police split
retains the existing 15% police chance. Refugee waves use the same scaled target.
Loading or reducing density does not add/remove existing residents. A native
generation check successfully places 300 residents on a 100x100 map.

Where the extra simulation capacity comes from
---------------------------------------------

Classic is the default and includes shared decision calculations, geometry-only
visibility reuse, bounded path-search storage and cheaper event recipient work.
Observers still check their own visibility, hearing, knowledge and relationships.

Responsive NPCs also lets safe civilians continue validated guarding, resting or
movement between full decisions, at most four local turns apart. Damage, danger,
orders, urgent needs, changed supplies and relevant events interrupt continuation.

Adaptive World includes Responsive NPCs and accounts for elapsed distant time.
Eligible quiet resting, guarding and isolated travel can use cheaper work; the
player's district, personal followers, combat, injury, fire, raids, active hunts
and awake predatory communities remain detailed. Real actors, inventories,
ownership, deadlines and dated information remain authoritative. Catch-up effort
and catch-up while waiting are saved options. Format 5 remains supported.

Measured results and limits
--------------------------

Prior Windows performance measurements used three sequential unprofiled repeats,
the same preserved controlled saves and actual changing-world turns. In a
crowded 200-actor fixture, median CPU round time was 236.439 ms on the preserved
baseline, 188.700 ms on optimized Classic, and 73.966 ms on Responsive NPCs:
3.20x baseline throughput for Responsive. The actual native offscreen command
cycle median changed from 223.012 to 118.518 ms, a 46.9% reduction. This does not
measure physical input latency, desktop composition or representative human play.

Quiet distant catch-up improved 24.35x in warmed comparisons and 4.90x in cold
processes. Dangerous maps stayed detailed and improved much less. The cold
result remains below the earlier 5x target; the warmed result does not replace it.

Separate generated-map population measurements show the cost of more residents.
At 100x100, the default ordinary target rises from the old fixed 25 to 100.
Median CPU round time was 6.886 ms at the old population, 25.215 ms for scaled
optimized Classic, and 23.858 ms for scaled Responsive. Responsive was about
5% faster there and slightly slower in smaller generated workloads. The 3.20x
crowded-fixture result is not a promise for every larger world.

The performance improvements provide headroom for larger populations, while
actual turn time still depends on population, danger, activity and hardware.
No new timing claim is made for the subsequent aggression feature. Its full
regression is 566/568; native units, 15 focused checks, genuine legacy loading
and the unchanged 10-second/50-MB save budgets pass. Generated diplomacy and
survival outcomes remain qualified in docs/community-aggression.txt.
